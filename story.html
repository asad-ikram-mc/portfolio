My story · Lahore to Portsmouth and back

I'm Asad Ikram. This is my story.

Data engineerChevening Scholar 2024/25Co-founder and CTO, Artemis AI

I came to computer science with no computing background and opened my Fiverr account in December 2018. That was the start of nearly eight years of data work: SQL tutoring first, then large-scale web and social media data collection, and the pipelines and models that turn that data into decisions people can trust. This page tells the story in order, from school in Lahore to the company I am building now.

  5. 05
    2021Chapter 05 · Final year

    Final year, first remote teams

    My final year was busy. From March to May 2021 I did a remote technology internship with Arthan, an Indian company: data mining, data analytics, data acquisition and lead generation, and automation that cut manual work. Between June and September I added the Google Data Analytics Certificate, Python Data Structures from the University of Michigan on Coursera, and HackerRank's Python and Problem Solving certificates. In August I reached Level 2 on Fiverr for the first time.

    For my final-year project, a team of three of us designed a Custom Network Simulator: a Python simulator of the Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) MAC layer with one-click dataset export and graphs, so researchers could generate training data for machine-learning models. Established tools such as NS2, NS3 and OMNeT++ are hard to extend with machine learning, and that was the gap. The idea was my supervisor Dr Bilal Khan's, and it sat far from my data work. I wanted to honour his idea and broaden my skills, which is exactly why I took it on. It taught me how network protocols work and how web traffic is handled.

    In November 2021 Propre, a Singapore-headquartered property-data company with a Japan office, hired me for a short remote contract as a data engineer. It was my first taste of organised remote work, and of real responsibility: the team worked on Discord, and it showed me how well an organised team can run when everyone is remote. After about three months I left to focus on my final-year project.

  6. 06
    2022Chapter 06 · Dubizzle Labs, Lahore

    A blank page at Dubizzle Labs

    My degree was conferred on 31 January 2022. I had accepted an offer from Dubizzle Labs while my final exams were still running, and on 1 February I started in Lahore as an Associate Software Engineer. Dubizzle Labs is the technology arm of Dubizzle Group, whose brands at the time included dubizzle and Bayut in the Middle East and Zameen and OLX in Pakistan. The group operates in about ten countries.

    The company wanted competitor analysis from public web data, and there was no architecture for it yet. With one colleague in the Dubai team, and guided by two mentors in the Pakistan team, I designed and built the first one on AWS: S3 as the data lake, Glue crawlers, Athena, and a Redshift warehouse in bronze, silver and gold layers, with dashboards in Periscope (Sisense).

    This is where I moved from collecting data to using it. I used machine learning for the first time there, estimating competitor growth and revenue from public data, and studying what people discussed, how much volume each site carried, and what worked on each platform and in each region. That summer I took five DataCamp courses in data engineering and Python to go deeper.

  7. 07
    2023Chapter 07 · Running the fleet

    300 crawlers, every day

    In February 2023 I was promoted to Data Engineer. The architecture had become a fleet: more than 300 crawlers running daily, covering six products across ten countries (Pakistan plus nine Middle East and North Africa markets), with the data maintained together with the Dubai team. I ran it, monitored it, extended it and kept crawlers repaired on time. To make it more reliable I added unit tests, CloudWatch alarms and Retool QA apps, built ElasticSearch pipelines, and tuned fact tables for warehouse performance.

    I also led the automation scripts that served marketing, finance, SEO and the CEO's team, led hiring for the scraping project, trained colleagues on ETL, and introduced juniors on my team to freelancing.

    In May 2023, with Dubizzle's written consent, I started a second, part-time remote role as a data engineer (independent contractor) for Fix.com in Canada, an online retailer of appliance and lawn-equipment repair parts and a division of Eldis Group. My job there is data acquisition: finding target sites, building crawlers for retail and parts catalogues, and automating manual work. Flows and pipelines I built in those first months still run. My manager there has been the same person since 2023, and he was later my employer referee for Chevening. Later I added OCR-based machine learning to read parts catalogues from PDFs and models to fill gaps in parts data.

    In November 2023 I sent my first Chevening application. At its centre was an idea I still work on: a cross-platform system for analysing public social-media discussion responsibly, so public bodies can read public reaction and make evidence-based decisions at the right time. My example was the business shutdowns in Karachi in September 2023, after fuel and electricity price rises were announced close together. In the same application I named Dr Ella Haig's teaching in Intelligent Data and Text Analytics as a reason to choose Portsmouth, before I had ever met her.

  8. 08
    2024Chapter 08 · Chevening

    Senior, selected, and on a plane

    In February 2024 I became Senior Data Engineer: two promotions in two years. I led a team of three engineers, two in web scraping and one in data warehousing, and the data-warehouse team for Propforce, Zameen's real-estate affiliate sales platform. I did its data modelling from scratch with Redshift, dbt, Matillion and Airflow, using slowly changing dimensions and star and snowflake schemas.

    The Chevening interview invitation came the same month, and I interviewed at the British Council in Lahore in March. I was conditionally selected in June, and my Final Award Letter arrived on 12 August 2024. I left Dubizzle that month, and in mid-September I moved to Portsmouth on my own to start an MSc in Data Analytics at the University of Portsmouth. New country, new city, new people. The Chevening orientation followed in November 2024, and through the University of Portsmouth's Student StartUp team I registered for Venturefest 2024.

    Chevening Scholar 2024/25

    Chevening is the UK Government's international scholarship programme, funded by the FCDO and partner organisations, and it has run since 1983. My award came with a contribution from the University of Portsmouth.

    I was selected on my first attempt, in a cycle of more than 70,000 applications worldwide (Chevening's figures imply about 72,000). Chevening awards around 1,500 scholarships a year to applicants from over 160 countries and territories; 46 went to Pakistan that year.

    From September 2024 I also worked part time and remotely for Prefe, a Milan start-up that provides price, promotion and distribution intelligence for consumer-goods brands, as one of its early engineers. I moved its web data collection onto Scrapy and built pipelines into its databases. In December, with the work done and my studies needing more of my time, we parted on good terms.

    That same December I won a part-time data engineering role at M+C Saatchi Fluency, the data consultancy of M+C Saatchi Group (a London-listed creative network with operations in about 23 countries), through a web-scraping challenge. Fluency has offices in London, New York and Singapore. I work remotely for the London team and daily with colleagues in New York, and I have also worked from the London office at times.

  9. 09
    2025Chapter 09 · Portsmouth

    A dissertation, a start-up, a Distinction

    The MSc is where I got much more interested in machine learning and AI. I worked hands-on with Apache Spark on the university's computing cluster and used it in my projects. I made friends from Italy, France, Spain, Greece, Nigeria, the USA and India, and I mentored classmates who arrived without a data background. My professors shaped where I am going: Dr Ella Haig, Dr Fahad Ahmad and Dr Rahim Taheri, the MSc programme coordinator.

    In February 2025 I co-founded Artemis AI in the UK as CTO, with a partner I met while living there. In April I registered for Chevening Ideas Connect: AI and Cybersecurity, in Lancaster. In June and July I was also Lead Data Engineer, part time, at VendueTech, a European proptech start-up, reporting to its CTO and leading three developers on real-time auction data pipelines and its Azure-to-AWS migration work with an AWS partner. I left when that migration project stopped, to focus on my MSc and my other work.

    My dissertation, supervised by Dr Ella Haig, was a cross-platform sentiment analysis of public reaction to UK economic policies: 255,446 public comments from X/Twitter, Reddit, YouTube and Quora, collected through official APIs and licensed data access, under university ethics approval and with user IDs hashed. I compared a VADER lexicon baseline, a BiLSTM and a fine-tuned BERT model, calibrated them, tested differences with McNemar and bootstrap tests, forecast trends with Prophet, measured emotion with the NRC lexicon, and explained predictions with LIME and integrated gradients. It earned a mark of 78.

    In September 2025 my study ended. I was welcomed into the Chevening alumni network, said goodbye at the Chevening Farewell at Alexandra Palace in London, and took ownership of Amazon's social-media analytics pipeline at Fluency. From late 2025 I also started working as a data engineer for CXG, a Dubai-based customer-experience consultancy, on data from global fashion and luxury brands. My MSc was awarded with Distinction on 8 December 2025, and that month I came home to Lahore, as Chevening scholars agree to do. Chevening has since confirmed that this is no problem for a PhD starting in 2027. During my year in the UK I went to events and made connections that still matter to me.

  10. 10
    Jan to Jul 2026Chapter 10 · Lahore

    Back in Lahore, still building

    Artemis AI Ltd was incorporated in the UK in January 2026. From Lahore I carried on with Fluency, Fix.com and Artemis, and went back to CXG for further engagements. One CXG problem I enjoy: the same product attribute is written differently by region and language, so "K18WG", "18K white gold" and "750/1000 white gold" have to become one shared vocabulary before anyone can compare brands.

    By February I was also co-leading ScrapeSync, a small web-scraping team. In May I started web-scraping-guide.com, a free, no-signup engineering guide to web data collection, including its legal and ethical limits. It is how I wanted to give back after my Chevening year.

    In June I became the sole designer and operator of Fix.com's crawler platform. ScrapeYard is a FastAPI control plane with a crawler registry, scheduling, live logs, on-demand data-quality checks, named logins with per-action audit trails, and Google Drive delivery. When a crawler breaks, AI can propose a fix, but it comes back as a diff that a person reviews; nothing commits on its own. It runs on an inexpensive VPS rather than managed cloud services.

    In mid-2026 I also began advising a US travel-technology company (under NDA) on its large-scale data-collection architecture. I have also helped Metoda, a company in Germany. And in July, with the Fluency team, I built a one-off collection across 20 powersports forums, plus Reddit through its official API.

  11. 11
    Aug to Oct 2026Chapter 11 · Agents and a paper

    AI agents, a preprint and PhD applications

    In August 2026 a developer advocate at Zyte got in touch because of the guide. Zyte's developer-relations team then interviewed me, invited me to review a pre-release version of its open-source agentic scraping toolkit, and later gave me two complimentary passes to the Web Data Extract Summit in Dublin.

    At Fluency, Crawler IQ, the system I built and own for Ford, is a fleet of more than 60 car-owner forum crawlers with two AI agents: a healer that diagnoses a broken crawler, regenerates it and tests it on live pages, and a builder that creates new crawlers from a brief. Their changes ship only after verification. By 9 October 2026, across 1,697 runs, the healer had fixed 86% of broken crawlers and the builder had succeeded on 92% of builds.

    I also own Amazon's CORE social-media analytics pipeline, whose master table holds about 2.2 million rows. In October I refactored it onto Fluency's shared data platform and checked parity file by file: all 138 exported files came out byte-identical. Alongside that, I built a 12-month US consumer discourse study for M+C Saatchi's New York team, with more than 100,000 consumer mentions, an 18-theme codebook that the team signed off, and a prompt version recorded on every machine label. I also support a UK government social-media analytics pipeline, and work on projects for global brands in consumer health, automotive, e-commerce and sport. Alongside the pipelines I build dashboards and internal websites for the team.

    14 October 2026 · first preprint

    My first preprint went live on arXiv (not peer reviewed), written with Dr Ella Haig and endorsed for arXiv by Dr Rahim Taheri: a protocol for checking whether platforms change what machine-labelled sentiment means. More in Research and PhD.

    The same month I applied to PhD programmes starting in 2027 in Europe, the UK and Singapore, with programmes in the US next on my list.

MSc dissertation · 2025

What my dissertation taught me

"Cross-Platform Sentiment Analysis of Public Reaction to UK Economic Policies", supervised by Dr Ella Haig. To check my labels I hand-labelled 200 posts myself; agreement with VADER was a kappa of about 0.78. The bigger lessons were about what I had got wrong:

  1. The classifier's apparent accuracy measured agreement with the lexicon that made its labels, not accuracy.
  2. I treated the platforms as if they measured opinion the same way. They do not have to.
  3. The English-only pipeline dropped posts written in Urdu and Hindi.

Together, those three lessons became the preprint and my PhD direction.

Preprint · not peer reviewed

Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?

Ikram, A. and Haig, E. (2026). "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? A Protocol and Simulation Study for Cross-Platform Equivalence of Machine-Labelled Policy Sentiment." arXiv:2610.16757. Version 1.1, 25 pages; cs.LG, cross-listed in cs.CL, cs.CY and cs.SI.

It treats each platform like a survey mode and sets out a six-step protocol:

  1. Fixed anchor events
  2. A generalizability study against human labels
  3. Measurement invariance and DIF
  4. A multilevel model
  5. Score linking
  6. A 500-replication known-truth simulation

The simulation shows that pooling sentiment across platforms misleads when platforms measure differently and the mix of platforms shifts, and that relative linking fixes it. The strongest headline comparison holds only when the true measurement functions are assumed known; with a realistic reference point the advantage shrinks. It has not yet been applied to real data. An experienced reviewer judged the idea sound but found the hand-labelled set and corpus from my MSc, which motivate it, small. That tells me what to fix next.

Next: I am planning further work with Dr Haig.

How it is built, layer by layer

  1. 01
    SourcesFacebook and Instagram Graph APIs, read-only.
  2. 02
    CollectionPosts, comments and insights; hourly growth curves for posts up to 14 days old; a 90-day backfill when a page connects.
  3. 03
    WarehouseApache Iceberg tables with an Athena star schema. Facebook and Instagram sit side by side and are never added together.
  4. 04
    ModelsOur own NLP models first, a large language model only below a confidence floor. Five DeBERTa-v3 comment models read each comment with its post; images go through OCR and CLIP; video through frames, speech-to-text and OCR.
  5. 05
    ServingPostgreSQL with pgvector, and a model registry.
  6. 06
    Art-ERetrieval over the page's own data with a safety guard. Every number must come from a checked tool, and unsourced numbers are blocked. An LLM judge runs on 200 test questions, and the next-move picker holds back 10% to prove it helps.
  7. 07
    AppiPhone and Android, hosted in AWS London.

Responsible by design

  • Read-only Meta permissions
  • Commenter IDs are never stored, and tagged names become "user"
  • Audience overlap kept only as page-pair counts of 5 or more
  • A UK-only data rule
  • A legal check that no AI vendor trains on customer content
  • Comment storms judged against each page's own normal

My part, and the team's

As CTO I lead the architecture, evaluation and QA dashboards, data governance and permissions, crisis definitions, and the planning and tracker. I set up the mobile app repository with CI and a protected main branch, and I take part in pitching and fundraising presentations.

I brought four contractors into the team, and our AI/ML and back-end engineers wrote most of the model pipelines. The whole company team is about eight people, including the co-founders.

What it taught me

Agreement between models is not accuracy, so our tracker calls it an accuracy proxy. Banter, dialect and non-English text are where models fail first, and our OCR reads only English. Those lessons are a big part of why I want a PhD.

The thread through all of it

Numbers from social media can be quietly wrong

  • LanguagePosts in other languages get dropped.
  • ToneBanter gets read as anger.
  • AccuracyAgreement between models gets passed off as accuracy.
  • PlatformsPooled platforms shift results when the platform mix changes.

My answer has two halves

Measure it, then build the checks in

MeasurementThe protocol in my preprint, and the PhD that should follow it.
EngineeringVerification gates on AI-repaired crawlers, versioned prompts, codebooks that people sign off, clearly flagged estimates, read-only data access and no stored commenter identities.

My personal goal, unchanged since my Chevening application, is to help public bodies read public reaction responsibly and decide at the right time. Today I pursue it through work like the UK government pipeline I support at Fluency.
